About Wiser Tools

Wiser Tools compares managed WordPress hosting for people who already know they need it and now have to pick one. This page explains what we check, where the information comes from, and — just as importantly — what we don't do.

What this site is

The hosting market doesn't have a shortage of "best hosting" lists. It has a shortage of lists that tell you which option fits your situation and where each one stops working.

So the comparison pages here are organised by the person buying, not by a ranking: agencies managing client sites, WooCommerce store owners, small businesses, and developers who want more control. Each host appears in the same four pages, but the recommendation changes with the reader — because that's the honest answer.

You will not find a single "overall winner" anywhere on this site. When a page has to give you one answer, that's usually a sign the page was written for a search engine rather than for you.

How we evaluate hosting

Every comparison is built from the same inputs, and the inputs are things you can check yourself:

  • Published plan documentation. Prices, visit limits, PHP worker counts, storage, staging, backup retention and migration terms, taken from each host's own site and help centre.
  • Policy terms that cost you money later. Renewal pricing versus intro pricing, what happens when you exceed a visit limit (throttle or overage), refund windows, and whether backups or object cache are included or add-ons.
  • Architecture, stated as architecture. Whether your site gets its own container, its own server, or a slice of a shared one — and what that means for uncached requests like WooCommerce checkout.
  • Who the plan is wrong for. Every host in a comparison gets a "this is the wrong choice if…" note. Limits are more useful than adjectives.

What we don't do

This is the part most review sites leave out, and it's the part that matters when you're judging whether to trust a number:

  • We don't run paid accounts on every host at once. We don't hold subscriptions with all the providers compared here, so we don't publish our own benchmark results. Where a figure would require us to have measured it, we say so and give you the documented spec or the architectural reason instead.
  • We don't publish invented performance numbers. Published TTFB figures vary enormously with test location, theme, plugin stack and cache state. A millisecond number we can't reproduce is worse than no number, so we leave it out.
  • We don't name a single winner, and we don't reorder a comparison because one host pays better than another.
  • Nobody pays for inclusion. A host isn't in a comparison because of a commercial relationship; the four providers on this site are there because they cover the range from budget shared hosting to premium managed platforms.

If a page here states something as a measurement, it will say who measured it and when. If it doesn't say that, treat it as a documented specification rather than a test result — that distinction is deliberate.

How this site is funded

Some pages carry affiliate links: if you click one and later buy, the host pays us a commission, at no extra cost to you. Those links are always marked with rel="sponsored" and the pages that carry them say so near the top.

Commission rates differ between hosts — sometimes significantly. That's a real conflict of interest, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest, so here's how it's handled: commission rate has no effect on which host is recommended for which situation. The comparisons are organised around plan limits and pricing, and those are facts you can verify on the host's own site in a couple of minutes. If you ever find a recommendation here that you can't reproduce from the provider's published terms, the page is wrong and it will be corrected.

Corrections and keeping pages current

Hosting plans, prices and limits change constantly, and a comparison that was accurate six months ago can be quietly wrong today. Pages are reviewed and updated on a rolling basis as providers change their plans. Anything that looks out of date almost certainly is — check the provider's own pricing page before you buy, which every comparison here also tells you to do.
