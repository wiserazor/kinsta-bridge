Managed WordPress Hosting Price Index

Every hosting advert shows the cheapest month of the cheapest term. Almost none of them record what you pay afterwards. This page is a dated record of both: first-term price, renewal price and three-year total for five managed WordPress hosts, read from each vendor's own pricing page on 2026-09-21.

Price indexes exist because memory is unreliable. A comparison written six months ago quotes plan names that no longer exist, at prices that no longer apply, and the reader has no way to tell. So this record carries a date on every row, names its sources, and gets re-published when the vendors move.

The reason renewal pricing gets its own column is simple: it is the number that decides your actual budget, and it is the number published in the smallest type. The gap between the two is where most "cheap hosting" decisions are lost.

Three-year totals assume one plan, unchanged capacity, no add-ons, no tax and no overage. hosting.com and SiteGround rows require a prepaid twelve-month term in year one. Kinsta's annual rate is ten months of monthly billing, which does not change at renewal. WP Engine publishes first-year pricing for new Essential customers and states that renewal pricing is subject to change, so no multi-year total can be computed from public information.

Two structures produce the sub-$500 totals, and they are opposites. hosting.com buys the position with the deepest first-year discount and a moderate renewal. Cloudways buys it by never discounting at all, which makes its price boring in the useful sense. SiteGround's entry plan has the cheapest year one and still finishes third, because its renewal is six times its intro rate.

What changed in this edition

Three of the five vendors changed more than a number, which is the reason this index exists in dated form rather than as a static comparison.

  • Kinsta renamed the whole lineup. Plans are now Business (Launch, Launch Plus, Build, Expand, Scale, Lead), Agency sized by install count (20, 40, 60) and Enterprise from $500/mo. Monthly rates run $35 to $299, and annual billing charges ten months for a twelve-month term.
  • WP Engine moved to Essential, Core and Enterprise. The pricing page now leads with these three and no longer lists the Startup, Professional, Growth and Scale line that most comparisons still quote. Essential is $336 per year billed annually, described as first-year pricing for new customers.
  • hosting.com's managed WordPress line is Small, Medium, Advanced and Turbo at $4.49 to $14.99 per month on a prepaid year, renewing at $9.99 to $34.99. Unusually, the monthly rate and the renewal rate are the same number, so the yearly term is the only discount.
  • SiteGround's renewal rates are higher than the widely repeated figures. StartUp renews at $17.99/mo and GrowBig at $29.99/mo, against $14.99 and $24.99 in material published earlier. Our own pages carried the older numbers until this check.
  • Cloudways upgraded the entry tier without changing the price. $11/month now buys 2 GB RAM, 50 GB storage and 2 TB of bandwidth, where it previously bought 1 GB, 25 GB and 1 TB.

The pattern worth taking away: plan names change as often as prices do. A comparison that quotes only prices can be wrong twice over.

How the numbers were collected

Each figure was read from the vendor's own pricing page in a browser on 2026-09-21, with the billing toggle switched to both the discounted term and the monthly term where the page offered one. Plan names, entry prices, renewal prices, billing terms and add-on prices were recorded together, because recording any one of them alone produced errors in the past.

Nothing here comes from a third-party review, a reseller page or an aggregator. That rule costs us coverage, because some vendors publish less than others, and it is why the WP Engine row says "unknown" instead of carrying a guess.

What this index does not contain is also deliberate: no performance numbers. No TTFB, no uptime percentage, no load-test results. We have not benchmarked these hosts on paid accounts, and a millisecond figure produced under somebody else's conditions tells you more about the test than the host.

Known gaps

  • WP Engine's renewal rate is not published, so its multi-year total cannot be computed. That is a gap in the vendor's disclosure, not in the collection.
  • Cloudways mid tiers are not all included. The vendor publishes a long list across five cloud providers; this edition covers the DigitalOcean entry, mid and top tiers.
  • hosting.com's shared hosting line is out of scope. This index follows the managed WordPress lineup that the comparison pages on this site actually discuss.
  • Regional pricing is not covered. Vendors serve different prices and currencies by country, and these are the USD rates the pages showed on the checked date.

FAQ

Why publish a price index at all?

Because renewal pricing is the least recorded and most consequential number in hosting, and because plan names change as often as prices. A dated dataset lets anyone check what was true on a given day instead of trusting a comparison of unknown age.

Are these the prices I will actually pay?

Not necessarily. Prices exclude tax, promotions rotate, and vendors serve different rates by region and currency. Treat the index as a map of how each vendor prices, then confirm the current figure on the vendor's own page before you buy.

Can I reuse this data?

Yes. It is published under CC BY 4.0, which means you can use it commercially, quote it and chart it, as long as you credit Wiser Tools and link to the index.

Why is WP Engine's three-year total "unknown"?

Because WP Engine publishes first-year pricing for new Essential customers and states that renewal pricing is subject to change. Without a published renewal rate there is no honest three-year figure, so the index shows the gap rather than filling it with an estimate.
