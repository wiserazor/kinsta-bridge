Why agencies need a different host

A freelancer judges hosting on price and speed. An agency judges it on operational risk. When a client's checkout dies at 9 p.m., the phone call comes to you — not to the host's support line. So the questions shift:

  • Can one noisy client site take down the others? (site isolation)
  • Can I bill hosting back to the client without it being a spreadsheet nightmare? (client billing)
  • Can I clone a site to staging, break things safely, and push to live? (staging & clone)
  • Can I move a site off my account and onto the client's without a migration weekend? (hand-off)

Shared budget hosting fails all four. That's why most agencies land on a managed host built for multi-tenant reality.

What actually matters for client work

1. Site isolation

Each client site should run in its own container or account, so a traffic spike or malware hit on one never touches another. Kinsta isolates per site in containers on Google Cloud C2; Cloudways isolates per server you provision. Both beat shared hosting, where "neighbor noise" is a real risk.

2. Client billing & white-label

The cleanest model is the host bills you at cost and you mark it up to the client — or the host supports reseller/client billing directly. Kinsta's Kinsta Agency program and WP Engine's agency tiers are built for exactly this; Cloudways has a reseller-style billing model too.

3. Staging & cloning

One-click staging is non-negotiable for client work: test the update, show the client, push to production. Kinsta's staging and "clone environment" are among the smoothest; Cloudways offers staging per app; WP Engine has a mature staging workflow.

4. Migrations & hand-off

Free migrations in both directions matter — bringing a client's old site in, and pushing a finished site out to their own account. Kinsta's site-transfer tool hands a site to a client's account while keeping your agency dashboard access (with their permission).

Cloudways for agencies

Cloudways is the "control" option: you pick the cloud (DigitalOcean, AWS, Google Cloud), they manage the stack, and you get a reseller-style billing model. Great when you have developers who want the knobs and clients who don't need hand-holding on infrastructure.

Pros: Lower entry cost; choose your cloud; flexible vertical scaling; reseller billing.

Cons: More WordPress-level tuning is on you; bandwidth overages surprise new agencies; steeper learning curve than fully managed.

WP Engine for agencies

WP Engine targets larger, brand-name clients and enterprise governance. If your roster includes big brands with compliance and plugin-policy requirements, its agency tiers and transferable installs are a fit — at a premium.

Pros: Enterprise trust; mature staging; transferable installs for hand-off.

Cons: Pricier at entry; stricter plugin policies; overkill for small brochure clients.

How we'd set up an agency stack

A pragmatic mix, not a single host for everyone:

  • Revenue-dependent clients (ecommerce, membership, lead-gen): Kinsta Business tier — origin performance for logged-in users matters.
  • Developer-heavy clients who want cloud control: Cloudways — you get the knobs without building the stack.
  • Enterprise / compliance clients: WP Engine — governance and brand trust.
  • A handful of small brochure clients: SiteGround — don't overpay for capacity they won't use.

Keep one "house" host (Kinsta for most) for operational consistency, and use the others where a client's needs genuinely diverge. That way your dashboard, your billing, and your support playbook stay predictable.

Tip: Standardize client onboarding — one staging checklist, one hand-off template, one "what's included" doc. The host handles infrastructure; your process handles the rest. The agencies that scale are the ones with a repeatable playbook, not the ones chasing the cheapest server.

FAQ

Can I bill Kinsta back to my clients?

Yes. Agencies typically mark up the cost in their monthly retainer, or use Kinsta's agency tooling to manage client accounts. The exact billing model depends on the plan you're on — verify current options on Kinsta's agency page.

How do I hand a site to a client?

On Kinsta, use the site-transfer tool to move a site to the client's own account while keeping agency access with their permission. No migration weekend, no DNS scramble.

Is site isolation really necessary?

For client work, yes. If one client site shares resources with another and gets hit by a spike or malware, isolation is what keeps the rest online. It's the difference between one angry client and five.

Which host is cheapest for a new agency?

Cloudways has the lowest entry price and a reseller model; SiteGround is fine for a few tiny brochure sites. But "cheapest" ignores the support and isolation you'll wish you had at 9 p.m. — price the operational risk, not just the server.

Do my clients need to be technical?

No. Managed hosting means staging, backups and PHP switching are one-click in the dashboard. You can hand a non-technical client a stable site and retain admin access as agreed.
