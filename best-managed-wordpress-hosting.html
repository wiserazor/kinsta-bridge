Why "managed" actually matters

Managed WordPress hosting means the host owns the boring, expensive-to-get-wrong parts: server-level caching, security patching, daily automatic backups, PHP version management, and one-click staging. You write content; they keep the lights on. The value isn't visible on a good day — it shows up precisely when a mistake is costly: a plugin conflict before a product launch, a malware scan at 2 a.m., or a traffic spike that would have taken down a bare-metal box.

With unmanaged hosting you're the one paged at 2 a.m. With managed, that's their on-call engineer. For most people running a real site, the monthly premium is cheaper than one hour of a freelance sysadmin.

Cloudways, WP Engine & SiteGround

Cloudways

Cloudways sits between raw cloud (DigitalOcean, AWS, Google Cloud) and fully managed. You choose the infrastructure; they manage the stack. Ideal if you want control and a lower entry price, but expect to do more of the WordPress-level tuning yourself.

Pros: Pick your cloud provider; flexible vertical scaling; pay only for the server you pick.

Cons: Steeper learning curve than fully managed; bandwidth can surprise you; more WordPress-level tuning yourself.

WP Engine

The enterprise-grade option — trusted by large brands and agencies. Strong for big sites, but the premium is real and some plugins are restricted.

The friendly on-ramp: cheap, fast onboarding, excellent support. Perfect if you're starting out and don't need enterprise scale.

Speed & performance, explained

Three numbers matter more than any "turbo" badge: TTFB (time to first byte — how fast the server responds), LCP (largest contentful paint — when the main content shows), and TTFB under load (does it hold up when traffic spikes). Kinsta's container isolation keeps TTFB stable under load because your container isn't sharing CPU with a surge on someone else's site. Budget shared hosts often win on a single cached request but fall apart exactly when you need them — during the traffic you paid for.

A CDN (Cloudflare on Kinsta, Bunny or Cloudflare elsewhere) handles static assets close to the visitor, but the origin TTFB is the floor everything else builds on. Cheap hosting with a great CDN still has a slow floor.

FAQ

Is Kinsta worth the price?

Only if performance or uptime touches your revenue. For a hobby blog, no. For anything mission-critical, the predictability pays for itself in avoided downtime and recovered hours.

Can I move my existing site?

Yes — Kinsta migrates sites from other hosts for free on plans above Starter, including DNS cutover and a staging check before you flip the switch.

What if I exceed the visit limit?

You're billed for overages and your site stays online. Watch visit counts in the dashboard and step up a tier before a campaign peaks.

Do I need to be technical?

No. That's the entire point of "managed." Staging, backups, and PHP switching are one-click in the dashboard.

Money-back guarantee?

Kinsta offers a 30-day money-back guarantee on all WordPress plans — low-risk way to test the dashboard and support yourself.

Tip: Start on the lowest tier that fits your traffic, then scale up. Managed hosts make upgrading painless — no migration weekend required.
